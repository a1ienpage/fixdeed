# Fixdeed — business evidence and pitch assumptions

Research checked 15 November 2026. Primary sources only. Facts, product proposals and modeled assumptions are separated below. This is pre-launch research: no interviews, customers, partners, savings, retention or traction have been supplied or independently verified. Team: extra moggers; Aliaskar (captain/coder), Zhamilya (economics/speaker).

## Pitch recommendation

**Fixdeed helps US first-time homeowners aged 25–45 turn a home problem into a safer next decision and a clearer spending plan during their first two years.** The Money-track story is avoiding poorly scoped decisions: identify urgency, record observations, compare what contractor quotes include, and send a consistent brief. Routine tasks and skill practice bring users back between problems. Do not promise quantified savings, diagnosis accuracy, lower contractor prices or prevention of damage without a study.

Proposed product: free safety triage, maintenance lists/repeat dates, all lessons/skill practice, repair ledger and personal JSON export; Plus at **$139.99/year or $14.99/month** for saved decision cases, quote scope comparison, contractor brief downloads and recurring ICS calendar exports. A $139.99 annual plan is equivalent to $11.67/month, billed $139.99 annually; twelve uninterrupted monthly payments total $179.88. Annual billing saves $39.89 / 22.18%. The demo has no separate family accounts or cross-device sharing. Quotes should be compared on scope, exclusions, materials, permits, warranty and timing, not ranked as a verified fair-price service.

“Live guided” should mean an interactive decision flow unless a staffed expert service actually exists. Frontdoor already provides human expert video calls. The economics below cannot fund unlimited human repair advice. Product functionality must be distinguished from proposed integrations, paid accounts and validated outcomes in the final demo.

## Market sizing without inflating the segment

**Measured context:** 87.749M owner-occupied homes is the nationwide all-age inventory, not SAM. Exact US households that are first-time owners, have owned for under two years, and have a target adult aged 25–45 were not established by these sources. Household count and adult count are different; charge one subscription per household and avoid multiplying by adults.

**Transparent cohort scenario, not a measured TAM:** NAR's release methodology cites **4.739M primary-residence purchases in 2025**. Applying the **2026 22% first-time share** to that older flow gives approximately **1.043M first-time purchases/year across all ages**. Assuming stable flow over two years gives 2.085M households before the age filter. The mix of periods, changing sales, household deduplication and ownership-duration transitions make this illustrative. If **50–70%** fall within 25–45 (an assumption still to validate), the first-two-year age-filtered cohort is **1.04–1.46M**, central 60% case **1.251M**. This cohort scenario does not establish obtainable revenue or willingness to pay. Do not present the 60% filter as a sourced demographic statistic. [NAR methodology and purchase baseline](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/first-time-home-buyer-share-inches-up-to-22-median-age-remains-at-record-high-of-40)

**Obtainable starting milestone:** validate 100 paying households ($13,999 annual-plan ARR), then target **1,000 paying households / $139,990 ARR** through measured channels. These are proposed milestones, not a market-share forecast or current traction. A local launch must count actual reachable qualifying households/closings before quoting a geographic SAM.

**Conditional expansion within US ages 25–45:** retain homeowners beyond their first two years while they remain in the age range; add richer home history, maintenance planning and contractor project records. EIA's **2024 RECS preliminary household table**, released **March 2026**, estimates **6.88M owner households ages 25–34 + 12.57M ages 35–44 = 19.45M**. This is a conservative age-band proxy inside the requested age range, excluding age 45, all tenure lengths, and based on the householder who answered the survey. It is neither first-time ownership count nor willingness to pay. EIA and HVS use different surveys/reference periods; do not combine their totals or imply they reconcile. [EIA Table HC9.2, PDF p.1 and notes p.2](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2024/hc/pdf/HC9.2_2024.pdf)

At the updated $139.99 annual price, 715,000 active annual-only subscriptions would yield $100,092,850 ARR. This is required-scale arithmetic, not a forecast, market-size estimate or willingness-to-pay evidence. A mixed active base would differ. The 60% annual assumption in the financial model describes new payers, not the retained active subscriber base. Cohort churn, aging out of the target segment, renewal and ongoing value beyond year two must be solved. See [financial assumptions](financial-model.md).

## Acquisition: practical experiments, not claimed benchmarks

NAR says **90% of buyers used an agent/broker** in the 2026 survey. That supports testing the closing moment as a channel; it does not prove partners will distribute Fixdeed or users will convert. HomeZada already markets buyer-agent closing gifts, providing a real distribution precedent rather than an exclusive route. [NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/first-time-home-buyer-share-inches-up-to-22-median-age-remains-at-record-high-of-40), [HomeZada buyer-agent offering](https://www.homezada.com/professionals/real-estate/buyers-agent)

| Proposed experiment | Why it fits / what to measure | Explicit decision rule (team-set, not benchmark) |
|---|---|---|
| Closing welcome kit through buyer agents and independent home inspectors | Invite a small number to offer a free personalized first-month plan. Track attributed invites → qualifying activations → first saved case/maintenance task → payment. No partners secured. | Continue only after at least 30 activated households provide usable feedback and fully loaded acquisition cost fits cohort contribution. Pilot enrollment is not traction until it happens. |
| Search pages for specific safe decisions and first-month maintenance | Intent is problem-led: e.g., “what to include in a plumber quote.” Publish reviewed practical material with a free decision flow. Track qualified traffic, activation, paid conversion, and time invested. | Test three topics; do not assume low CAC or organic scale before measurement. Avoid claiming SEO is free: value author/reviewer time. |
| Short demonstrations and homeowner-community feedback | Show a task, decision case and brief in one minute. Recruit only with community permission; avoid spam. Track completion and actual paid intent. | Compare the guided flow against a free checklist on time-to-next-action and comprehension, not social impressions. |
| Referrals after useful exports | A household can share a maintenance plan or contractor brief. Sharing is a proposed product loop, not evidence of viral growth. | Measure attributed activations/payments and any incentive cost. Do not forecast a viral coefficient. |

A useful first 6–8 weeks: 20 discovery interviews and 10 observed task sessions; 30 activated target households; then offer both real prices and seek 10 genuine payments with clear cancellation/refund terms. All numbers are proposed research targets. Record reasons for refusal and return usage at day 7/30. Validate comprehension and appropriate escalation separately from conversion; no safety outcome claims from a small usability pilot. Track cohort renewal and actual variable cost before scaling paid acquisition.

## Updated financial model

The team supplied a revised model on 15 November: $14.99/month or $139.99/year, annual contribution $106.25, 24-month blended contribution LTV $108.92, target blended CAC $34.50 and modeled contribution payback approximately 4.2 months. All are planning assumptions, not measured outcomes. The 24-month model keeps annual and monthly renewal behavior separate, then blends new payers 60%/40%. Full costs, channel budgets, fixed $8,000/month allowance, sensitivity and source links are in [financial-model.md](financial-model.md). ## Suggested ten-slide evidence spine

1. Product and target: first two years, US adults 25–45; one clear money decision.
2. Problem: recurring upkeep plus unclear contractor scope; sourced maintenance incidence and FTC guidance.
3. Why this customer: NAR 22% and median 40, with period on slide.
4. Working demo: problem → urgency → saved case → comparable scope → brief.
5. Repeat value: maintenance plan, practice and calendar export; show implemented behavior only.
6. Competition: HomeZada, Frontdoor, Dwellin and free checklist; admit overlap and test focus.
7. Reachable launch: initial 100 then 1,000 paying households; no invented traction.
8. Price and economics: $139.99 annual / $14.99 monthly, $106.25 modeled annual contribution; explicit assumptions.
9. Distribution validation and conditional scale: closing kits, search intent, measured CAC; $100M subscriber arithmetic is an ambition.
10. Team and next milestone: Aliaskar builds, Zhamilya validates economics/presents; request a measurable pilot opportunity rather than claim market proof.

### Claims to keep out

“No competitors”; “first AI home-care app”; “all 87.7M homes are our target”; “most first-time buyers are 25–45” without age distribution; “saves $X per household”; “prevents emergencies”; “verified fair quote price”; “human experts included”; fake interviews, waitlists, pilots, partners or revenue; revenue inferred from downloads or test/demo records. The strongest current claim is an inspectable product and an explicit plan to validate demand.
